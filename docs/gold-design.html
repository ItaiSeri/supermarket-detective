1 · Lineage

From the chains' websites to a chart

Raw files are kept untouched in S3. Bronze holds every value as published, silver cleans and keys it, and gold shapes it for questions. The big tables stay in Databricks, and Power BI imports only the small gold marts.

2 · Gold tables

A small star, plus one mart per question

The star (3 dimensions, 2 facts) is the only thing that reads silver. Every mart reads only the star, so keys, filters (physical store, shared barcode) and business rules live in one place, and two charts can't disagree because they took a shortcut to silver. A fact is stored as a table only when building it does real work (changes the grain, or is too expensive to repeat); otherwise it's a view over silver. Marts are always tables, rebuilt daily. The one exception is ops_health, which reads pipeline metadata, not prices.

Store-wide promotions (a promotion listing more than 10,000 products: coupons, donations, delivery fees) stay in silver and are not in gold: they are not deals on products (decided 2026-11-17). Product deals have one row per store × promotion × group × product. A promotion ID is the deal's identity, so the row is never split by its values: chains re-encode the same deal from day to day (Shufersal's 1+1 as 50% / 19.90 one day, 100% / 0.00 the next). The last day's values are kept, and values_changed flags a deal whose values differed. A product in two promotions at once gets two rows.

3 · First finding
0.847

Median price of the same product in the same chain, Eilat vs the rest of the country, today (current prices at physical stores). 1 / 1.18 = 0.847, so the 18% VAT exemption reaches the shelf in Shufersal (0.847), Mahsani Ashuk (0.847) and Rami Levy (0.855). In Carrefour the median is 1.000, no discount at all on most products. That's the first post, once Carrefour's case is checked.
